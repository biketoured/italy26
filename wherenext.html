Where next?

Italy took forty days and a year of learning Italian before it. What comes after is still open — five journeys across 2027 and 2028, mostly three weeks each, one of them a race, and two of them a language I do not speak yet. None of it is decided.

There is a thread running through most of this list, and it is not the riding. Italy began with Italian; two of the journeys below begin with Greek and with French. A country opens up in proportion to how much of it you can ask for, and a bicycle happens to be the slowest and therefore the best way to find that out.

01

Bosnia · Serbia · Romania · Bulgaria · Turkey

The overlooked half of Europe, ending in Istanbul

East and south through the countryside nobody puts on a poster: Bosnian river valleys, Serbian farm roads, the Carpathians, the Bulgarian mountains, and then the long run down to the Bosphorus. This is the part of Europe that tourism forgot, which is exactly why it is on the list — villages where a cyclist is still a curiosity rather than a traffic problem, and where the coffee you get invited to is not optional. It ends in Istanbul, which may be the most spellbinding city on the continent, and is the right kind of full stop: three weeks of quiet countryside and then fifteen million people at once.

Distance

~2,300 km

Climbing

~25,000 m

Length

3 weeks

Window

May–Jun or Sep

The catch Five countries, four languages and two alphabets in twenty-one days, with border queues that do not care about the schedule. The Balkan interior bakes from July onward, the road surface is genuinely unknown until you are on it, and the same emptiness that makes it special makes resupply a thing you have to plan rather than assume.

02

Chile & Argentina · Patagonia

Final Frontier Patagonia — Puerto Montt to Ushuaia

The only one on the list with a start gun. Final Frontier Patagonia is an unsupported ultra-distance event that runs the length of Patagonia — the Carretera Austral, Ruta 40, Torres del Paine and Tierra del Fuego — from Puerto Montt in Chile to Ushuaia at the bottom of the world. No support crew, no resupply plan but your own, twenty-one days maximum for the full course. Every other trip here is about arriving slowly; this one is about finding out what happens when there is a clock on it.

Distance

2,700 km

Format

Unsupported race

Time limit

21 days

Window

March

The catch It is the only journey here you can actually fail. A March start sits nowhere near a Swedish holiday, the flights and the bike box cost more than the rest of the list put together, and the Patagonian wind is not a detail — it is the whole event. The shorter finisher stages (650, 1,619 and 2,250 km) exist for a reason.

03

Greece · autumn 2027

Greece, in Greek

The same experiment as Italy, run again in a harder language. Spend the year learning as much Greek as I possibly can, then go and spend it: three weeks in the autumn through the Pindus mountains, past Meteora, down the Peloponnese and out to whichever islands the ferries allow. Autumn is the point — the heat has broken, the summer crowds have gone home, the sea is still warm, and the people who are left have time to talk. That last part is the whole reason for learning the alphabet.

Distance

~1,800 km

Climbing

~27,000 m

Length

3 weeks

Window

Sep–Oct 2027

The catch Greek is not Italian — a year of evenings gets you polite, not fluent, and the alphabet is the easy part. Greece is also far steeper than its reputation: the Pindus goes up at gradients that Italy mostly had the decency to switchback. And once islands are involved the ferry timetable writes the route, not me.

04

France · 2028

All in on France

The big one, and the direct sequel to Italy: the same formula, a year of French beforehand, and a route that refuses to skip a region. Brittany and the Atlantic, the Pyrenees, the Massif Central, Provence, the Alps, and home along whichever coast is left. France is the country that invented taking a bicycle seriously, and it is large enough and varied enough to deserve six weeks rather than three. If Italy was the proof that this works, France is the version done properly the second time.

Distance

~4,000 km

Climbing

~50,000 m

Length

5–6 weeks

Window

Summer 2028

The catch It needs what Italy needed: six weeks away from work, a year of language before it, and a full winter of route-drawing. That makes it the least spontaneous thing on the list and the easiest one to postpone by a year — and doing it half-heartedly, in three weeks, would miss the entire point of going all in.

05

Morocco · the Atlas

The green Atlas, in the Swedish dark

The exact opposite of home, taken at the exact moment home is at its worst. Northern Sweden in late autumn is dark by three in the afternoon; the Atlas at the same time of year is green from the rains, the valleys are full of olive and almond, and the light lasts. Three weeks out of Marrakesh over the high passes, through mud-brick villages and gorges, with a mountain range that turns red twice a day. Not a summer trip escaping to the sun — a winter trip escaping the lack of it.

Distance

~1,600 km

Climbing

~22,000 m

Length

3 weeks

Window

Nov–Feb

The catch Green depends entirely on rain that may not come, and the high passes in winter can hold snow and close without warning — the valleys are mild, two thousand metres up is not. Daylight is short at that time of year everywhere, the piste eats tyres, and water has to be thought about every single day.

Five journeys, two years, and no decision yet.

If you have ridden any of these, or you know the one road on them that must not be missed — or you speak Greek and have an opinion about how I should be learning it — I would very much like to hear about it before the maps get folded away.
